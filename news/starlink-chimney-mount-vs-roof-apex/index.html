Starlink Chimney Mount vs Roof Apex Bracket UK: Best Fitting

How to mount Starlink safely on British slate and pantile roofs. Non-drill chimney lashing cradles vs gable apex storm brackets. Prevent tile damage and ensure clear satellite line of sight.

By Gary, ADI Communications · 2026 · 3 min read

Quick answers

Will installing a Starlink dish on my chimney crack the brickwork?

No, because we do not drill into chimney brickwork. We use professional CAI-standard double stainless steel lashing cradles that loop high-tensile wire rope around the entire stack, distributing mechanical load evenly without puncturing mortar.

Can Starlink be mounted directly onto roof tiles?

Never directly through roof tiles. Drilling through slate or concrete tiles shatters the tile and creates water leak points. If a roof mount is required, we use special roof hook brackets that slip under the slates and bolt directly into structural rafters.

How high should the dish be mounted above the chimney pot?

The Starlink dish should be mounted at least 30cm to 60cm above the highest chimney pot to ensure hot smoke and sulfurous fumes from log burners do not coat the antenna radome or melt plastic joints.

What is a gable apex bracket?

A gable apex mount (such as a heavy-duty Pennine bracket) fixes to the solid timber wall plates or brickwork at the triangular peak of your roof gable, keeping all hardware off the tiled roof surface.

How do I know if my chimney stack is strong enough for Starlink?

During our initial site survey, our engineer inspects the mortar condition and stack stability. If mortar is crumbly or the stack is leaning, we immediately recommend an apex gable mount to avoid placing strain on the chimney.
